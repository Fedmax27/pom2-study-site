Week 11

Rheumatology begins. Monday is the hot joint: hand infections (cellulitis vs a deep infection, bites, paronychia, felon, Kanavel's signs), septic arthritis (the aspirate, the organisms, cefazolin and a washout), osteomyelitis (sequestrum, MRI, vertebral OM) and the diabetic foot, then the rheumatologist's acute monoarthritis: infection first, then gout and pseudogout, their crystals and their treatment. Tuesday widens to many joints: classifying polyarthritis by time, count and pattern, the extra-articular clues, RF, anti-CCP and ANA (and when not to order them), synovial fluid and x-rays; then the same questions in children with juvenile idiopathic arthritis: its seven subtypes, silent uveitis, growth and treatment. Thursday covers the spine and the commonest arthritis: ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic and reactive arthritis, then osteoarthritis (a clinical diagnosis, staged on x-ray, treated with exercise first). Friday is systemic disease: vasculitis (giant cell arteritis and ANCA vasculitis) and the connective tissue diseases (lupus, antiphospholipid syndrome, Sjögren's, myositis, systemic sclerosis and Raynaud's).

Monday

Nov 9 MSK infections, then acute monoarthritis.
11.1

MSK Infections

  • Cellulitis vs deep hand infections, cat and fight bites, paronychia, felon and Kanavel's four signs; septic arthritis (history and exam, the monoarthritis differential, the aspirate, hematogenous seeding, S. aureus and strep, washout and cefazolin; gonococcal, Lyme, TB, viral, reactive and prosthetic joints); osteomyelitis (biofilm, sequestrum, who gets it, MRI, OVIVA, the vertebral OM case); the diabetic foot (why, assessment, probe to bone, swabs, imaging, antibiotics by severity)→
11.2

Acute Monoarthritis

  • “Infection, infection, infection, gout, pseudogout”; aspirate the red joint for the 3 C's; septic arthritis risk factors, organisms and I&D; gout (the sink, who, SMARTS triggers, needle-shaped negatively birefringent crystals, normal urate in an attack, steroid/NSAID/colchicine/prednisone, allopurinol and tophi); pseudogout (rhomboid positive crystals, the knee, chondrocalcinosis)→

Tuesday

Nov 10 Polyarthritis in adults, then in children.
11.3

Approach to Polyarthritis

  • Inflammatory vs degenerative, acute vs chronic, mono/oligo/poly; onset and course (episodic, migratory, additive); the four distribution patterns and which joints each disease favours; risk factors and viral arthritis; extra-articular features of RA, psoriatic and reactive arthritis, gout, OA, SLE, dermatomyositis and scleroderma; CBC, CRP, RF, anti-CCP, ANA and ENA, lupus complement; the synovial fluid table and crystals; inflammatory vs degenerative x-rays; DMARD classes→
11.4

Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis

  • The four criteria and red flags; the seven subtypes (oligo and its painless limp, poly RF−/RF+, systemic with quotidian fever, salmon rash and MAS, enthesitis-related, psoriatic, undifferentiated); leg length, the jaw and silent anterior uveitis with slit-lamp screening; pGALS; the workup (ANA for uveitis risk, advanced maturation on x-ray); treatment by course (naproxen, injections, methotrexate, biologics, IL-1/IL-6 blockers); the two cases and six practice questions→

Thursday

Nov 12 Spondyloarthritis, then osteoarthritis.
11.5

Seronegative Arthritides

  • The SpA family; inflammatory vs mechanical back pain; ankylosing spondylitis (HLA-B27, modified New York and ASAS criteria, sacroiliitis, bamboo spine, non-radiographic axial SpA, enthesitis, extra-articular disease); psoriatic arthritis (five patterns, nails, dactylitis, pencil-in-cup, PsA vs RA); reactive arthritis; Schober, chest expansion and occiput-to-wall; NSAIDs, TNF and IL-17 inhibitors and what works where→
11.6

Osteoarthritis

  • Chronic monoarthritis differential; OA as a clinical diagnosis (no MRI); Kellgren–Lawrence staging; 1 in 7 Canadians; primary vs secondary OA and risk factors; the “sick joint”; the four questions; exercise, weight loss, topical NSAIDs, duloxetine and injections; what doesn't work; arthroplasty vs arthroscopy→

Friday

Nov 13 Vasculitis, then connective tissue disorders.
11.7

Vasculitis

  • Vessel wall inflammation, ischemia and aneurysm; secondary causes first (hepatitis, HIV, cocaine, cancer, CTD); classification by vessel size; when to suspect it and the workup (urinalysis and chest x-ray for everyone); the purpura/hepatitis C case; giant cell arteritis (symptoms by artery, vision loss, ESR/CRP, biopsy and halo sign, prednisone now); ANCA vasculitis (GPA, MPA, EGPA, c- vs p-ANCA, features, biopsy, admit vs nephrology)→
11.8

Connective Tissue Disorders

  • The CTDs and their prevalence; a lupus case; SLE criteria (2019 points and ACR 1982), rashes and organs; epidemiology and treatment (hydroxychloroquine for all); antiphospholipid syndrome; Sjögren's; inflammatory myositis (mimics, dermatomyositis signs, anti-Jo-1, cancer); systemic sclerosis (criteria, limited vs diffuse, the 15% rule, renal crisis); Raynaud's; match the CTD→
