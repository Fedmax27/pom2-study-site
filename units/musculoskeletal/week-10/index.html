Week 10

The lower limb finishes and the clinical topics take over. Monday reads hip, knee and ankle x-rays; grades and localizes nerve injuries (neurapraxia to neurotmesis, root vs nerve, what an EMG can tell you); and works through the ankle and foot: the joints, ligaments, arches and pulses, then sprains and the Ottawa rules, Achilles and plantar fascia problems, tibialis posterior dysfunction, bunions, Morton's neuroma and the diabetic foot. Tuesday describes fractures on x-ray (OLD ACIDS), manages them (reduction, open fractures, healing, compartment syndrome and the other complications) and works up a soft tissue lump without the “whoops” of shelling out a sarcoma. Thursday is chronic pain: wind-up and central sensitization, active vs passive therapy, the drugs and the opioid START principle, then the psychology (fear-avoidance, mood, somatic symptom disorders, CBT). Friday (physical activity prescription and pediatric orthopedics) will be added as it's covered.

Monday

Nov 2 Lower limb x-rays, nerve injury, then the foot and ankle.
10.1

Lower Limb Radiology

  • The systematic read and TIMS-C; the hip series, Shenton's line and the AP pelvis lines, femoral neck fracture, arthroplasty and OA; the knee's AP, lateral and skyline views, Blumensaat's line, effusions and lipohemarthrosis, bipartite patella, TKA and ACL reconstruction; the ankle mortise, Kager's fat pad, malleolar fractures, spurs and enthesophytes→
10.2

Nerve Injury

  • Nerve layers; neurapraxia, axonotmesis and neurotmesis, Wallerian degeneration and 1 mm a day; radiculopathy vs mononeuropathy, dermatomes and myotomes, the numb thumb (carpal tunnel or C6); what nerve conduction and needle EMG can and can't show; foot drop at the fibular head vs L5; the three practice questions→
10.3

Clinical Approach to the Foot & Ankle

  • Tendons of the dorsum and sole; the talocrural, subtalar and transverse tarsal joints; the lateral and deltoid ligaments and the spring ligament; the arches and weight bearing; arteries, pulses and the foot's nerves; Achilles tendinopathy and rupture, plantar fasciitis, ankle sprains, the Ottawa rules and malleolar fractures, tibialis posterior dysfunction, bunions, Morton's neuroma and the diabetic foot→
DSSG

Nerve & Soft Tissue Injury: Review Sheet

  • Dr. Cassidy's eight cases (ulnar nerve at the elbow, neck pain to C6 radiculopathy to rotator cuff, mechanical back pain, GTPS, radial nerve after a humerus fracture, foot drop after a hip replacement, PFPS, brachial plexus injury), each question linked to the site section that answers it, plus quick facts the modules don't cover→

Tuesday

Nov 3 Reading fractures, managing them, and soft tissue tumours.
10.4

Fracture Radiology

  • The read (anatomy, trace the cortex, look for an effusion) and OLD ACIDS; AP, oblique and lateral plus the mortise and Y-views; epiphysis to diaphysis and the apophysis; transverse, oblique, spiral, longitudinal, comminuted (butterfly, segmental) and the incomplete childhood fractures; distal-relative-to-proximal (except the clavicle), varus vs valgus, translation and off-ended, rotation, distraction, impaction and override; intra-articular fractures, steps and the fat-fluid level; open, periprosthetic, avulsion and pathological fractures→
10.5

Fracture Management

  • Resuscitate, reduce, hold, re-x-ray, rehab; closed reduction step by step; ORIF, nails, external fixators and casts; open fractures (antibiotics, tetanus, splint, debride, irrigate, fix, close) and the open tibia case; primary vs secondary healing, the stages and the timelines; VTE, compartment syndrome, nonunion and malunion, AVN, post-traumatic arthritis and heterotopic ossification→
10.6

MSK Oncology: Soft Tissue Tumours

  • Benign vs malignant (100 to 1), causes and the benign/malignant names; how the pathologist makes the diagnosis (core biopsy, IHC, MDM2); history and exam of a lump; the <5 cm / >5 cm / suspicious-features guideline that avoids the “whoops”; biopsy types; staging with a CT chest, the tumour board, wide excision and pre-op radiation; the four cases→

Thursday

Nov 5 Chronic pain: the biology and drugs, then the psychology.
10.7

Understanding and Managing Chronic Pain

  • Fibres, the dorsal horn, NMDA wind-up and the medial and lateral systems; gate control, endogenous opioids and descending NE/5-HT; acute, subacute and chronic, nociceptive vs neuropathic; fear of harm and its effects; the pain-clinic team; passive vs active therapy; NSAIDs, acetaminophen, TCAs and SNRIs, gabapentinoids and cannabinoids; opioids and START (ORT, urine screens, agreements, special groups, the 90 MED watchful dose, switching and tapering)→
10.8

Psychological Factors in Chronic Pain

  • Fred's case; assessment, pain scales and the pain diary; the six dimensions of pain and the fear-avoidance model; adjustment disorder, depression, anxiety and substance use; medically unexplained symptoms, somatic symptom disorder, conversion and factitious disorder vs malingering; multimodal management, CBT and its distortions, pacing, relaxation and secondary prevention→
