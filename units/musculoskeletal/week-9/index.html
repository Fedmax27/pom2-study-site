Week 9

The upper limb wraps up and the lower limb begins. Monday reads shoulder, elbow and wrist x-rays, then finishes the hand: the median and ulnar nerves' branches and lesions, carpal tunnel syndrome and ulnar neuropathy at the wrist, plus the upper extremity quiz. Tuesday moves to the hip: its bones, ligaments and blood supply, and the approach to a hip fracture; then the gluteal region and the three compartments of the thigh. Thursday works through hip pain by location (osteoarthritis, labral tears, avascular necrosis, trochanteric pain, piriformis) and the knee's bones, ligaments and menisci and their injuries. Friday covers the four compartments of the leg, then anterior knee pain, bursitis, Baker's cysts, injections and the approach to knee pain. Monday afternoon is the distal upper extremity lab; Friday's lab is the hip and thigh.

Monday

Oct 26 Upper limb x-rays, then the nerves of the hand.
9.1

Upper Limb Radiology

  • A systematic read (maturity, bone, alignment, joints, soft tissue) and TIMS-C; the shoulder's AP, axillary and Y views, OA, surgical neck fractures and dislocation; the elbow's fat pads, anterior humeral and radiocapitellar lines; the carpal bones and the Gilula arcs→
9.2

Clinical Approach to the Wrist & Hand, Part 2

  • The median and ulnar nerves in the hand and their territories; hand of benediction, claw hand and ulnar canal syndrome; Erb and Klumpke palsies; the carpal tunnel; then carpal tunnel syndrome (causes, night symptoms, C6 vs CTS, the night splint) and ulnar neuropathy at the wrist; and the upper extremity quiz, worked→

Tuesday

Oct 27 The hip joint and hip fractures, then the gluteal region and thigh.
9.3

Clinical Approach to the Hip Joint

  • The hip bone, femur and leg bones; why the hip is so stable (fit, spiral ligaments, labrum); the femoral head's blood supply and avascular necrosis; dislocation; then hip fractures: who breaks a hip, the shortened externally rotated leg, neck vs intertrochanteric vs subtrochanteric, arthroplasty or nail within 48 hours, and preventing the complications→
9.4

Anatomy of the Gluteal Region & Thigh

  • The sciatic foramina and piriformis; the gluteal muscles, Trendelenburg gait and safe injections; the sciatic nerve; the anterior, medial and posterior compartments with their nerves; hamstring avulsion, groin pull and popliteal aneurysm; the popliteal fossa, femoral triangle (NAVEL), femoral hernia, the great saphenous vein and the lymph nodes→

Thursday

Oct 29 Hip pain by location, then the knee's ligaments and menisci.
9.5

Clinical Approach to Hip Pain

  • Where "hip" hurts (groin, lateral, buttock); hip osteoarthritis (C sign, internal rotation lost first, FABER, Stinchfield, FADIR, x-ray vs symptoms, treatment ladder); labral tears and MR arthrogram; avascular necrosis and steroids; trochanteric bursitis vs pain syndrome, single-leg stance and the socks question; piriformis syndrome and its tests; the snapping hip→
9.6

Clinical Approach to the Knee, Part 1

  • The knee's bones, x-ray views and capsule; the collateral ligaments and what the ACL and PCL stop; ACL, PCL, MCL and LCL injuries (mechanism, tests, treatment, return to play); the menisci, their blood supply and the screw-home mechanism; meniscal tears, degenerative vs traumatic, and the unhappy triad→

Friday

Oct 30 The leg's compartments, then knee pain and its approach.
9.7

Anatomy of the Leg

  • Leg and foot bones on drawings and x-rays; the four compartments, their jobs and nerves, compartment syndrome and shin splints; dorsiflexors, evertors, gastrocnemius vs soleus (calf raises) and the tarsal tunnel (Tom, Dick and Harry); the tibial and fibular arteries, venous return and varicose veins; the fibular nerves, foot drop and the skin map→
9.8

Clinical Approach to the Knee, Part 2

  • The Q-angle and what keeps the patella on track; patellofemoral pain syndrome; knee bursitis, the Baker's cyst and IT band friction syndrome; corticosteroid, hyaluronic acid, PRP and stem cell injections; the history, swelling before or after 6 hours, and the differential by location, with the emergency department knowledge check→
