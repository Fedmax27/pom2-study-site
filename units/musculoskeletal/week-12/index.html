Week 12

Rheumatology wraps up and neurology begins. Monday is two review modules: how rheumatologists treat (prednisone doses and side effects, joint aspiration and injection, NSAIDs, the four DMARDs and their toxicities, biologics and when not to use a TNF inhibitor) and which blood tests to order and when not to (RF, anti-CCP, ANA, anti-DNA, ENA, complement, ANCA). Tuesday starts the neuro block with neuroanatomy (lobes, deep grey matter, the brainstem and the rule of 4, cranial nerves, tracts, ventricles, vessels) and the neurologist's approach (UMN vs LMN, patterns of weakness, time course and VITAMINS C&D), and closes rheumatology with non-arthritic body pain: fibromyalgia and polymyalgia rheumatica. Tuesday’s neuro modules live in the new Neurology unit.

Monday

Nov 16 Two rheumatology review modules.
12.1

Rheumatology Treatment

  • Prednisone by dose (the high-dose four, PMR, gout, RA flares), routes and the 15 side effects; aspiration and injection (consent, C3G, contraindications); NSAIDs, COX-2 and their heart/gut/kidney risks, acetaminophen, opioids; treating RA early and to target; methotrexate, hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine and leflunomide side by side (GHMI); biologics and TNF inhibitor contraindications→
12.2

Rheumatology Investigations

  • No diagnosis by blood test alone; RF and anti-CCP; ANA (1 in 3 women, when to order, never repeat), anti-dsDNA, ENA (Ro, La, Sm, Scl-70, Jo-1) and complement; ANCA (c vs p, levamisole); expected labs in RA, SLE, SpA, JIA, GCA, PMR and fibromyalgia→

Tuesday

Nov 17 Neuro begins; rheumatology ends with body pain.
12.3

Introduction to Neuroanatomy

  • CNS vs PNS, grey and white matter; directions, planes and CT/MRI (T1, T2, FLAIR); lobes and their jobs, limbic lobe, basal ganglia, diencephalon; midbrain, pons and medulla with their landmarks; the rule of 4 and where each cranial nerve leaves; spinal cord horns and tracts; ventricles and CSF flow, the blood–brain barrier, the middle meningeal artery, the arterial circle and dural sinuses→
12.4

Introduction to Neurology

  • The vocabulary (plegia, paresis, aphasia, ataxia); neuron to cord; the motor pathway and UMN vs LMN signs; the homunculus and MCA vs ACA; sensory and visual pathways; the four-step approach, how to localize, patterns of weakness, time course and VITAMINS C&D; four practice cases (migraine aura, chronic subdural, cervical myelopathy, Parkinson’s)→
12.5

Body Pain: Fibromyalgia and PMR

  • Nociceptive vs neuropathic vs central pain; fibromyalgia criteria (WPI and SS), not a diagnosis of exclusion, the two pain pathways, multimodal treatment (exercise, CBT, duloxetine, pregabalin, amitriptyline, no opioids); PMR (over 50, girdle stiffness, high ESR/CRP, thrombocytosis, no weakness), the steroid test, the taper and bone protection→
