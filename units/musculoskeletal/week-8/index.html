Week 8

From the neck down to the fingertips. Monday builds the brachial plexus, the map every upper-limb nerve lesion is solved with, then the muscles of the shoulder and arm, with rotator cuff disease and biceps tendinopathy. Tuesday adds the arteries, veins and nerves of the arm (and a 10-question shoulder quiz), then the bones of the forearm, wrist and hand, and the elbow's joint, ligament and bursa problems. Thursday is the forearm muscles, with tennis and golfer's elbow, distal biceps rupture and mallet finger. Friday is the forearm's vessels and nerves, with ulnar neuropathy at the elbow, then distal radius and scaphoid fractures and the anatomy of the hand. 8.1 and 8.2 are the preparation for Tuesday's Part I lab; 8.3 and 8.4 for Thursday's Part II lab.

Monday

Oct 19 The brachial plexus, then the muscles of the shoulder and arm.
8.1

Brachial Plexus

  • The ventral rami of C5–T1 from roots to terminal branches; the branches at each level; why the cords are named around the axillary artery; the “M”; the terminal nerves' roots, muscles and skin; and how to draw it→
8.2

Clinical Approach to the Muscles of the Shoulder

  • The pectoral muscles and serratus anterior, with the winged scapula; the deltoid, the four rotator cuff muscles and teres major; then rotator cuff disease: intrinsic versus extrinsic causes, the exam (Neer, Hawkins, painful arc), stages I–III to cuff arthropathy, physio first, and when to repair or replace→
8.3

Clinical Approach to the Muscles of the Arm

  • The two compartments and their nerves; biceps (the supinator), brachialis (the elbow flexor), coracobrachialis and triceps; then proximal biceps tendinopathy: why the long head fails, the exam and the Popeye deformity, the differential for anterior shoulder pain, and management→

Tuesday

Oct 20 The arm's vessels and nerves, then the distal bones and the elbow.
8.4

Neurovascular Supply of the Proximal Upper Extremity

  • Subclavian to axillary to brachial; the three parts of the axillary artery and the scapular anastomosis; the brachial artery, BP and fracture risk; the cephalic, basilic and median cubital veins; the nerves of the arm and their skin; and the module's 10-question shoulder quiz, worked→
8.5

Osteology of the Distal Upper Extremity & Clinical Approach to the Elbow

  • The elbow's bones, hinge, ligaments and carrying angle; the radioulnar joints and pronation; the carpals and the hand; then elbow OA, posterior dislocation, nursemaid's elbow, UCL injury and Tommy John, and olecranon bursitis, benign or septic→

Thursday

Oct 22 The forearm muscles and the elbow's tendon problems.
8.6

Clinical Approach to the Muscles of the Distal Upper Extremity

  • The forearm's two compartments: flexor-pronators from the medial epicondyle (median nerve but for 1½) and extensor-supinators from the lateral (all radial); every muscle layer by layer; the thumb, the snuffbox and wrist movements; ganglion cyst and mallet finger; then tendinitis vs tendinosis, tennis and golfer's elbow, distal biceps rupture, and eccentric strengthening→

Friday

Oct 23 The forearm's vessels and nerves, then the wrist and hand.
8.7

Clinical Approach to the Neurovascular Supply of the Distal Upper Extremity

  • The radial and ulnar arteries and the elbow collaterals; the cubital fossa (TAN); the median, ulnar and radial nerves in the forearm, with their sites at risk (pronator syndrome, the funny bone, Guyon's canal, supinator, the snuffbox); sensation of the hand; and ulnar neuropathy at the elbow, from Tinel's sign to the towel splint→
8.8

Clinical Approach to the Wrist & Hand, Part 1

  • The hand's joints; distal radius fractures (crooked, short, smooth; reduction, casting and surgery); scaphoid fractures (retrograde blood supply, be suspicious, cast or screw, nonunion and necrosis); then the palm's fascia and carpal tunnel, the thenar, hypothenar, lumbrical and interosseous muscles, the digital sheaths and the palmar arches→
