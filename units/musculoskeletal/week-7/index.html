Week 7

The first MSK week. Thursday lays the foundation, then takes on back and neck pain. First the vocabulary used for the rest of the block: position and movement, the spinal nerve, dermatomes, contractions and joints. Then mechanical pain (worse with movement: discs, facets, muscles, whiplash) and non-mechanical pain (radiculopathy, stenosis, cauda equina, myelopathy and the red-flag conditions). Friday is the bones and joints of the shoulder, and reading spine x-rays. Thursday's 7.2 and 7.3 are also the preparation for Friday's neck and back anatomy lab.

Thursday

Oct 15 The MSK vocabulary, then back and neck pain, mechanical and not.
7.1

Introduction to MSK Anatomy

  • The concept reference for the whole block: anatomical position, planes and directional terms; the terms of movement; the generic spinal nerve, dorsal and ventral rami, and the reflex arc; dermatomes and myotomes; isometric, concentric and eccentric contractions; open and closed kinetic chains; synovial joints, Hilton's law and what makes a joint stable; the skeleton and bony terminology→
7.2

Clinical Approach to Mechanical Back & Neck Pain

  • Mechanical pain (worse with movement, better with rest) versus the red flags; the vertebral column, a typical vertebra and its joints, and how cervical, thoracic and lumbar vertebrae differ; the ligaments; degenerative disease; discogenic versus facetogenic pain and disc herniation; the back muscles and myofascial pain; whiplash; and who needs imaging→
7.3

Clinical Approach to Non-Mechanical Back & Neck Pain

  • The spinal cord, nerve roots and meninges; radiculopathy, which root a disc hits, and the dermatome, myotome and reflex for C5–C8 and L3–S1; lumbar stenosis with neurogenic claudication, and cauda equina; cervical myelopathy; inflammatory back pain; spondylosis versus spondylolysis versus spondylolisthesis; visceral pain, infection and tumour→

Friday

Oct 16 The shoulder's bones and joints, and how to read a spine x-ray.
7.4

Osteology & Joints of the Proximal Upper Extremity

  • The pectoral girdle and its four joints, and scapulohumeral rhythm; the clavicle, scapula and humerus; the AC and glenohumeral joints; AC osteoarthritis versus AC separation (types I–V); shoulder arthritis, read from active and passive motion; instability, traumatic (a torn labrum, Hill–Sachs) versus AMBRI; and frozen shoulder→
7.5

Axial Spine Radiology

  • The ABCS approach; the normal C-, T- and L-spine and sacrum view by view, with the four lines, ADI and prevertebral soft-tissue limits; TIMS-C; C-spine fractures and dislocations and the stability rule; spondylosis, retropharyngeal abscess, isthmic spondylolisthesis and the Scotty dog; ankylosing spondylitis and sacroiliitis; hardware, the Cobb angle and normal variants→
