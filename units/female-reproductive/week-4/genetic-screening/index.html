    Genetic Screening for Pregnancy in Canada

    If You Remember Nothing Else
    • Screening is not diagnosis. NT, eFTS, the anatomy scan and NIPT all give a risk. Only CVS or amniocentesis confirms. A positive NIPT still needs an invasive test.
    • Primary prevention = the history. Take a 3-generation pedigree. The red flags are consanguinity, developmental delay or autism, congenital anomalies, ≥3 miscarriages, infertility (including male CBAVD → CF), early heart disease or sudden death, neonatal death, and ethnicity.
    • Ethnicity → carrier screen: European → CF. Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, Asian and African → thalassemia and hemoglobinopathies (MCV <80, electrophoresis, start with the mother). Ashkenazi Jewish → Tay-Sachs, Canavan, familial dysautonomia. French-Canadian (Bas-St-Laurent, Gaspésie, New Brunswick) → Tay-Sachs, 1/14. Anabaptist → the LHSC community panel.
    • Fragile X / FMR1: a full mutation (>200 CGG) causes fragile X syndrome in boys. A premutation (~55–200) causes POI before 40 in women and FXTAS (tremor and ataxia, often mislabelled Parkinson's) in older men.
    • Timeline: NIPT ≥9 wk → NT and eFTS 11–13+6 wk → CVS 11–13 wk → amnio 15–20 wk → MSS 16–20 wk → anatomy scan 18–20 wk. Termination is an option only before 24 weeks.
    • NT >3.5 mm → aneuploidy (T21, T13, T18, 45,X), congenital heart disease, Noonan syndrome, skeletal dysplasia.
    • NIPT (placental cell-free DNA in the mother's blood) is funded in Ontario for previous trisomy, maternal age ≥40, NT >3.5 mm or positive eFTS. It detects about 99% with <1% false positives. It screens T21, T13, T18 and the sex chromosomes only, not single-gene disease.
    • CVS vs amnio: CVS is earlier but carries a higher loss risk, plus maternal cell contamination and confined placental mosaicism. Amnio samples fetal cells, so neither problem applies.
    • A Robertsonian translocation trisomy means testing the parents. A balanced carrier parent means high recurrence risk, so offer PGT or early prenatal diagnosis next time and cascade testing to relatives.
    • Never assume, never judge, offer every option. Religion does not predict what a couple will choose.

    Fragile X and the FMR1 Spectrum

    Three Presentations, One Gene

    Fragile X is the most common single-gene cause of developmental delay in boys. The inheritance is X-linked. The gene is FMR1, and the mutation is a CGG triplet-repeat expansion. The repeat size decides which condition you see:

    CGG repeatsCategoryClinical picture
    6–44Normal—
    45–54Intermediate—
    55–200PremutationWomen: POI, with raised FSH and menopause before 40 of no other known cause.
    Men: FXTAS, a late-onset tremor and cerebellar ataxia that is often misdiagnosed as Parkinson's.
    >200Full mutationFragile X syndrome: developmental delay, large head, long face, prominent forehead and chin, protruding ears, joint laxity, and large testes after puberty.
    Why the POI History Matters in Preconception Counselling

    A woman with unexplained POI before 40 may be a premutation carrier. If she conceives before her ovaries fail, the repeat can expand to a full mutation in her child, and a son will have fragile X syndrome. So early menopause in a female relative counts as a red flag, even though it doesn't sound like a genetics question.

    Part 2 — Why Offer Prenatal Screening (and Why Not)

    Screening is offered, always optional, and couples can opt out.

    Her Example

    She describes a mother with an abnormal screen or an uncertain ultrasound who spent the rest of the pregnancy overwhelmed, and then delivered a healthy baby. That is the cost of a false positive, and it belongs in the counselling before the test, not after.

    Nuchal Translucency

    A trained sonographer measures the fluid behind the fetal neck. Excess fluid reflects trouble with the heart or lymphatics, and that is why the associations are:

    • Aneuploidy: T21, T13 (Patau), T18 (Edwards), and 45,X (Turner). Turner fetuses can become frankly hydropic.
    • Congenital heart defects.
    • Single-gene causes: Noonan syndrome, skeletal dysplasias.

    What NIPT Cannot Do

    NIPT screens only the common aneuploidies (T21, T13, T18) and the sex-chromosome aneuploidies. It is still a screen, so a positive result needs CVS or amniocentesis to confirm it. It says nothing about single-gene conditions such as NF1, Noonan syndrome or Huntington's. That point is exactly what the module's knowledge check tests (below). Because it tests only the mother's blood, there is no miscarriage risk.

    Part 3 — Invasive Testing: CVS and Amniocentesis

    Invasive testing gives a definitive diagnosis. It can detect aneuploidy and other chromosomal imbalances, or provide fetal DNA for single-gene testing when the family mutation is known (for example, Marfan syndrome in a parent). Maternal-fetal medicine specialists perform both procedures under ultrasound guidance.

    Work Backwards from What the Family Wants

    The questions are: what are we suspecting, how long does each test take, and what will the family do with the answer? The answer might mean termination (which must happen before 24 weeks), NICU planning, or palliation and taking the baby home. If NIPT would take 10 days and a positive would still need an amnio plus 2 more weeks, it can make more sense to go straight to amniocentesis.

    Values, Beliefs and Ethics

    • Culture, personal and religious beliefs, plus personal and family experience with genetic diagnoses, all shape choices. A family that has already had two trisomies may not want a third.
    • Never assume. She has seen religious Amish, Mennonite and Muslim couples choose termination, and couples told the fetus had T13 or T18 who declined all testing and carried to term. Religion does not predict the decision.
    • Offer all options to everyone, regardless of background. Never judge.

    Preimplantation Genetic Testing (PGT)

    PGT means testing IVF embryos before transfer and implanting only unaffected ones. Her example is a couple with Huntington's disease in the family: autosomal dominant, a 50% risk in every pregnancy, progressive and untreatable, so they are unwilling to take the chance. The technique was first developed in the late 1980s, when PCR was used to determine the sex of embryos in carriers of X-linked disorders.

    • Testing methods: PCR or multiplex PCR for single genes, and FISH or microarray for translocations and de novo aneuploidy.
    • Cost: the first IVF cycle is funded, but the PGT itself is not and costs about $6,000–15,000.
    • Caveat: the few cells biopsied may not represent the whole embryo. Trophectoderm becomes placenta. So she recommends confirming with invasive testing once the pregnancy is established.

    Why Test the Parents Here but Not for Classic Trisomy 13

    Free trisomy 13, with three separate copies, comes from nondisjunction, and testing usually stops there. A translocation trisomy can be inherited from a balanced carrier parent, so both parents are tested. Balanced carriers often present with recurrent miscarriage, because unbalanced conceptions are lost. That is why ≥3 miscarriages is a red flag in Part 1.

    "Lethal" Is the Wrong Word

    Trisomy 13 and 18 are often called lethal, but not every affected baby dies at birth. Technology can sustain life for long periods, and she has a 3-year-old patient with trisomy 13. Families understand "lethal" in different ways, and a lethal diagnosis does not automatically mean no intervention.

    Summary

    • Offer prenatal screening to every pregnancy, after a full discussion of risks and benefits.
    • Couples can opt in or out.
    • Every couple has a 2–3% chance of a baby with a congenital anomaly regardless of screening. Screening can reassure, but it never guarantees.

    Check Yourself: the Module's Knowledge Check

    Alice is 29 with NF1, confirmed by a molecular test that found her pathogenic mutation. She knows it is dominant, with a 50% recurrence risk. She is now pregnant and wants prenatal diagnosis.

    1. Which test should be offered to confirm the baby's status?
    Why

    Amniocentesis provides fetal DNA to test for her known familial mutation. eFTS and NIPT screen only for aneuploidy, not single-gene conditions. NF1 has no known prenatal ultrasound findings.

    2. When can that test be offered?
    Why

    Amniocentesis is done at 15–20 weeks. Option C, 11–13 weeks, is the window for eFTS and CVS. CVS would also work for a known mutation, but it wasn't offered as an option here.
