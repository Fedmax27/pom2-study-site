    Abortion in Canada

    If You Remember Nothing Else
    • There is no federal abortion law in Canada. Since R v Morgentaler (1988) it is treated like any other medical procedure. Access is guaranteed by the Canada Health Act but varies hugely by province, especially the gestational age limit.
    • Most abortions are early and done outside hospitals. In 2022 there were 97,211: about 79% in non-hospital clinics, 60.5% surgical and 39.5% medical. No referral is needed.
    • Medical abortion = mifepristone + misoprostol (Mifegymiso), up to 70 days (10 weeks), 95–98% effective. Mifepristone blocks the progesterone receptor (decidual necrosis, cervical softening, prostaglandin sensitivity); misoprostol is a PGE1 analogue that ripens the cervix and contracts the uterus.
    • Surgical: MVA up to 10 weeks, D&C (suction) up to 14 weeks, D&E in the second trimester. Cervical prep with misoprostol or osmotic dilators. 98% of abortions have no complication at all; the commonest are hemorrhage, infection and retained products.
    • Abortion is far safer than continuing the pregnancy (0.567 vs 7.06 deaths per 100,000) and does not cause infertility, breast cancer or mental illness. Ovulation can return 2 weeks after, so start contraception immediately: an IUD or Depo can go in the same day.

    Learning Objectives

    • Review pregnancy termination options in Canada.
    • Compare options based on gestational age and describe the risks of abortion.
    • Compare the risks of termination with the risks of pregnancy.
    • Outline counselling about fertility and contraception.

    The Law: How Canada Got Here

    YearWhat happened
    1969Abortion permitted only in a hospital, if a committee of doctors decided the pregnancy endangered the woman's health or life. Strict criteria, long delays
    1982Charter of Rights and Freedoms: any law contravening it can be struck down
    1988R v Morgentaler: the Supreme Court strikes the abortion law down as unconstitutional, violating section 7 (life, liberty and security of the person)
    1989After a boyfriend obtained an injunction against Chantal Daigle, the Supreme Court rules that a father or male partner has no veto
    1990Bill C-43 (two years' jail for providers where health was not at risk) passes the Commons but dies on a tie vote in the Senate
    1995Rulings force Nova Scotia and New Brunswick to allow private clinics, but coverage outside hospitals stays inconsistent
    Nov 2014New Brunswick drops the rules that an abortion must be done by an OB/GYN and certified in writing by two physicians
    Jun 2015Abortion added to the inter-provincial billing agreement, so care is portable like every other procedure
    Jul 2015Mifepristone approved by Health Canada (not actually available until 2017)
    Jan 2017Abortion services available on Prince Edward Island for the first time
    TodayNo federal law at all. Abortion is legal, treated like any other procedure, governed by provincial and medical regulation

    Two things to take from the timeline. Most abortions in Canada are provided by family physicians in standalone clinics, not by specialists in hospitals. And the barriers that were lifted recently — two-doctor certification, paying out of pocket in another province — were lifted within the last decade.

    Access: It Depends Where You Live

    • Access is guaranteed by the Canada Health Act, but availability and the gestational limit vary across a very big country.
    • In Ontario, OHIP covers abortion in both hospitals and clinics. Surgical abortion is provided up to about 24 weeks; induction abortion beyond 25 weeks exists at only one centre and is extremely limited. Beyond that, patients may be referred to cooperating clinics in the United States.
    • Where the limits differ between the lecture's table and the spoken Ontario figure (23+6 vs 24+6), they reflect moving targets that change as services change.

    When, and With What Complications

    Two-thirds of abortions happen in the first trimester (30.4% at or before 8 weeks and 35.6% between 9 and 12 weeks, in the 2017 hospital data), and the numbers fall steadily with advancing gestation.

    Before Any Abortion

    Part 1

    Medical Abortion

    Pills, at home. The first-trimester option that has changed Canadian practice since 2017.

    Choosing Medical or Surgical

    Mifepristone and Misoprostol

    DrugWhat it isWhat it does
    Mifepristone (RU-486)Anti-progestin, a derivative of norethindroneBinds the progesterone receptor without activating it. Progesterone is required to maintain an early pregnancy, so blocking it causes decidual necrosis and bleeding. Also softens the cervix and increases sensitivity to prostaglandins, raising the frequency and amplitude of contractions (peak at 36–48 h) and lowering the threshold for uterine contractility. Some effect within 15 minutes
    MisoprostolProstaglandin E1 analogueCervical ripening: less collagen, more soluble collagen, more matrix metalloproteinases, collagen degradation. Uterine contraction: inhibits adenylyl cyclase → less cAMP → more calcium → increased uterine tone
    Methotrexate (older option)Antimetabolite blocking dihydrofolate reductaseBlocks DNA synthesis, acting on the cytotrophoblast rather than the embryo, so implantation stops. Used up to 49 days. Teratogenic (cranial malformations, palsies, growth delay, limb deformities), so follow-up is essential
    The "Abortion Reversal" Claim

    Some providers claim that high-dose progesterone after mifepristone can reverse it. It can't. The patient is still making just as much progesterone as before; the problem is that the receptor is blocked. Mifepristone is all-or-nothing at the receptor: flooding the system with progesterone does not undo it. Changing one's mind after taking it is, in any case, very uncommon given the counselling beforehand.

    Part 2

    Surgical Abortion

    Manual vacuum aspiration, D&C in the first trimester, and D&E in the second.

    Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA)

    • Up to 10 weeks gestation.
    • Paracervical block with 1% lidocaine at a minimum; other sedation offered if available.
    • Most patients, especially nulliparas, need some cervical dilatation.
    • Transilluminate the tissue to confirm chorionic villi and a gestational sac, or do a post-procedure ultrasound to confirm an empty uterus.
    • Failure rate 0.25%.
    • Good for: office setting, immediate, private, local anaesthetic only, cost-effective. Not for: contraindications to local anaesthesia, a patient who can't cooperate with the exam, or very young or distressed patients who may need sedation.

    Dilatation & Curettage (Suction D&C)

    Vacuum or suction aspiration up to and including 14+0 weeks, using a rigid cannula on an electric suction machine. It can be done under local with a cervical block, but conscious or moderate sedation is usually offered, and general anaesthetic in some hospital settings.

    1
    Bimanual exam: confirms size and expected gestation, and tells you whether the uterus is anteverted or retroverted so instruments go the right way and don't perforate.
    2
    Speculum, prep and paracervical block (lidocaine, sometimes with vasopressin and/or epinephrine to reduce blood loss). Pre-operative antibiotics are given for a therapeutic abortion.
    3
    Mechanical dilatation only if needed. If cervical prep was adequate, skipping it means less discomfort and less infection, because fewer instruments pass through the vagina into the uterus.
    4
    Suction curettage to remove all products. Sharp curettage is unnecessary in the vast majority of cases and carries a higher complication rate, including Asherman syndrome.
    ComplicationRecognize it byDo
    HemorrhageMore likely with uterine atony, a low implantation or a larger gestational ageA repeat curettage may be needed to confirm everything is out
    Hematometra (post-abortal syndrome)Blood collecting in the cavity: excruciating pain, an enlarging uterus, little or no bleeding, often urinary retentionRe-evacuate immediately

    Advantages of D&C over medical abortion: less risk than general anaesthesia (it can be done with conscious sedation), faster recovery, much less bleeding afterwards, quicker discharge without required follow-up, high patient acceptance and cost-effectiveness.

    The lecture's own point: the greatest obstacle to safe D&E in Canada is the shrinking number of skilled practitioners. That is starting to turn around, helped by a second-trimester providers group formed during the pandemic so that clinicians know who offers what.

    Part 3

    Counselling: Risk, Contraception and Fertility

    The parts of the visit that happen before and after the procedure itself.

    Compared With Continuing the Pregnancy

    Contraception Afterwards

    • Ovulation can return as early as 2 weeks after a first-trimester abortion, so contraception should start immediately if wanted.
    • Every method is appropriate: having an abortion is not a contraindication to anything. Review all of them.
    • Timing: a pill, patch, ring or Depo-Provera can start the same day as a surgical abortion, or on the day of the day-3 bloodwork after a medical one. An IUD or implant can go in during the surgical procedure (painless under sedation), or at the two-week visit once a medical abortion is confirmed complete. Expulsion is slightly higher when an IUD is placed immediately (3.8%), but still under 5%.
    • If a method failed, offer a different one — and work out why it failed: cost, trouble swallowing or remembering pills, fear of needles, a partner who won't use condoms, or a medical reason to avoid estrogen (previous DVT, migraine with aura, smoking over half a pack a day over age 35). IUDs have their own limits, such as a uterine malformation or the cost of insertion and removal.
    • If the procedure is done by a surgeon under general anaesthetic and the patient has completed childbearing, a tubal ligation can be offered at the same time.

    Fertility and Emotions Afterwards

    • The most common emotional reaction is relief, sometimes with transient guilt, sadness or a sense of loss. Overall, patients are content with their decision.
    • A safe abortion carries no greater risk to fertility than a D&C for miscarriage.
    • Asherman syndrome: adhesions form in the endometrial cavity, sometimes causing secondary amenorrhea. Risk factors are uterine curettage (about 8% of cases), repeat curettage (most often for retained products or retained placenta postpartum), and infection. The slide puts the risk at up to 30% with a third consecutive procedure.
    • Trauma is the major cause of intrauterine adhesions; the role of infection is controversial and hard to separate out, because infected products usually mean retained products, which mean another procedure.
    • After a single, uncomplicated first- or second-trimester procedure, the risk is very low.

    The Myths, Answered

    MythThe answer
    Abortion causes breast cancerNo evidence. The only biologically plausible thread is that breastfeeding slightly lowers breast cancer risk, so not carrying a pregnancy to term means not breastfeeding — which is no different from never being pregnant, or choosing not to breastfeed
    It's used as birth controlThe decision is almost never taken lightly. Patients needing repeat abortions are often experiencing reproductive coercion, financial hardship, isolation, abuse or trafficking, or have medical contraindications to contraception (or believe the myths about it)
    It's illegal in CanadaNo. There is no federal law. (US law is a separate, shifting picture; very few patients cross the border for care here)
    It causes infertilityNo, as above
    Only single women have abortions80% are unmarried, so 1 in 5 are in a committed relationship, and 60% already have children
    Women from religious backgrounds don'tAbortion doesn't discriminate by culture, religion, ethnicity, income, relationship status or parity
    Products of conception are used in makeup, vaccines or unethical researchNot accurate. Where tissue is used in research, it is with ethics approval and the patient's explicit, informed permission
