    Drugs Used in Gynecology

    If You Remember Nothing Else
    • Everything follows from two facts about the HPO axis: GnRH, LH and FSH are pulsatile, and sex hormone levels follow a temporal pattern. Give continuous, supraphysiologic hormone and you wreck the pattern — that's contraception.
    • Drugs here do one of three things: disrupt the HPO axis (contraception), oppose overgrowth of hormone-dependent tissue (breast, endometrial, prostate cancer), or replace what's missing (menopause, hypogonadism).
    • The progestin does the contracepting; the estrogen controls the bleeding. Which progestin matters: estranes and gonanes are androgenic (acne, hair), drospirenone is anti-androgenic but has mineralocorticoid activity (K+ retention).
    • SERMs and SPRMs are agonist, partial agonist or antagonist depending on the tissue, because each tissue has different receptor isoforms and different co-factors. Tamoxifen: antagonist in breast, partial agonist in endometrium (so, endometrial cancer risk). Raloxifene: antagonist in both, agonist in bone. Clomiphene blocks the hypothalamus, which releases FSH/LH and induces ovulation.
    • For periods: NSAIDs block prostaglandins (pain and less bleeding); tranexamic acid is an antifibrinolytic that stops clots being dissolved — more effective for blood loss, but contraindicated with a history of thrombosis.

    • Identify the benefits, risks, side effects and contraindications of contraceptive preparations.
    • Explain how selective estrogen and progesterone receptor modulators are used in breast cancer and endometriosis.
    • Describe the pharmacological uses of androgen agonists and antagonists.

    The Principles

    Two concepts, three therapeutic strategies. Everything else is detail.

    Physiology First: Estrogen and Progesterone

    Both hormones work through nuclear receptors (ERα/ERβ, PR-A/PR-B) that regulate gene transcription. Hold on to that: it's the whole explanation for how SERMs can be agonists in one tissue and antagonists in another.

    What's Actually in Them

    Why the Family Tells You the Side Effects

    A progestin's structure decides not just how well it binds the progesterone receptor but how much it hits the other steroid receptors.

    Pregnanes (progesterone-like) are the most selective progestationally. Estranes (testosterone-like) are the most androgenic → acne, oily scalp, facial hair, weight gain. Gonanes are androgenic but less so. Drospirenone is anti-androgenic (good for acne) but has mineralocorticoid activity → fluid and salt retention and a risk of hyperkalemia. All of it is dose-dependent, which is why the same progestin appears with different profiles at different doses.

    So pick the progestin for the goal: acne → anti-androgenic; opposing estrogen in menopause → high progestational activity.

    Side Effects, Predicted From Physiology

    The Serious Risks

    Net effect across cancers: combined contraceptives are associated with a decreased risk of ovarian, endometrial and colorectal cancer.

    Drug interactions to remember: smoking alters estrogen clearance through CYP enzymes, and carbamazepine, rifampin and aprepitant can lower estrogen levels enough to matter. Common antibiotics do not.

    Part 3

    SERMs & SPRMs

    The same molecule acting as agonist, partial agonist or antagonist depending on which tissue it lands in.

    How Tissue Selectivity Works

    Three things decide the outcome: which receptor isoform the ligand prefers, what shape it forces the receptor into, and which co-factors that tissue happens to express.

    Pure antagonists exist too: fulvestrant binds, blocks and degrades the estrogen receptor, shutting signalling down completely; it's used in advanced breast cancer, including tumours resistant to tamoxifen. Aromatase inhibitors take a different route: less androgen→estrogen conversion means less estrogen to begin with.

    SPRMs: From Fibroids to Emergency Contraception

    DrugWhat it does
    MifepristoneMostly anti-progestin and anti-glucocorticoid. Blocking endometrial PR lowers hCG support and detaches the pregnancy → termination (see 4.1). The anti-glucocorticoid action makes it useful in Cushing syndrome
    Ulipristal acetateA partial agonist used for emergency contraception and fibroids

    Neither is an abortifacient. Once a pregnancy is established, they don't work. The clinical side of emergency contraception, including the copper IUD, is in 4.3.

    Part 4

    Androgens & the Drugs for Periods

    The other half of the module: androgen agonists and antagonists, NSAIDs and tranexamic acid.

    Androgens

    Aromatase inhibitors appear here too, treating breast cancer, endometriosis and gynecomastia — and, as seen in 4.2, inducing ovulation (letrozole).

    NSAIDs and Tranexamic Acid

    NSAIDsTranexamic acid
    DoesInhibit COX → fewer prostaglandins → less pain and less bleeding. Most women get moderate to excellent pain reliefAntifibrinolytic: prevents clot breakdown, so more effective than NSAIDs at reducing blood loss
    Side effectsGI upset (prostaglandins protect gastric mucosa), renal damage (they maintain renal perfusion)Headache in up to 60%, backache, cramps, GI distress
    Avoid ifRenal failure, gastritis or peptic ulcer, coagulation disorders (COX makes thromboxane, a platelet aggregator)History of thromboembolic events
    NoteBoth are symptomatic: they treat the bleeding and pain, not the underlying cause. Tranexamic acid is usually taken for a few days, or only on heavy days
