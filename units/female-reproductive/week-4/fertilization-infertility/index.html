    Fertilization & Infertility

    If You Remember Nothing Else
    • Fertilization happens in the ampulla. Sperm reach the tube within 5 minutes, must capacitate there first, and the egg is only viable for 12–24 hours. Sperm survive up to 5 days but fertilize best for 2–3.
    • Infertility = no pregnancy after 1 year of regular unprotected intercourse. It affects 10–15% of couples. 85% conceive by 1 year, 93% by 2.
    • Work up at 1 year if under 35, at 6 months if 35 or older, and right away if you already know there's a problem (e.g. PCOS with irregular cycles — they won't conceive without ovulating).
    • Infertility is 50% female and 50% male (female 41%, male 24%, both 24%, unexplained 11%), so always investigate both partners. A semen analysis is cheap and comes first.
    • The standard work-up: day 2–4 bloods (FSH, LH, estradiol, prolactin, TSH — always together), a sonohysterogram on days 6–12 for the cavity and tubes, day 21 progesterone to prove ovulation, a semen analysis, infectious disease screening, and AMH (which measures egg reserve, not natural fertility).

    Learning Objectives

    • Explain how fertilization occurs.
    • Describe the optimal time for intercourse to achieve a pregnancy.
    • Outline an approach to infertility for a family physician.
    • Outline general causes of infertility, including prevalence.
    • Describe an approach to a sensitive assessment of fertility concerns.
    • Describe populations who require early referral for fertility concerns.
    • Outline how pelvic infection or pain affects fertility.

    Part 1

    Fertilization

    Sperm transport, capacitation, egg pick-up, and the sequence from binding to a diploid zygote.

    Sperm Transport

    StepWhat happens
    EjaculationActive sperm rapidly enter the uterus; the seminal plasma stays in the vagina. Contractions of the female tract help sperm into the cervical mucus
    TransportSperm can be at the site of fertilization within 5 minutes
    Capacitation (in the tube)Three changes: ability to bind the zona pellucida · capacity to undergo the acrosome reaction · hypermobility
    Two Things to Reassure Patients About

    "It all fell out when I stood up." That is seminal fluid. The sperm are already gone from the vagina and into the uterus.

    "Should I lie down for 30 minutes after?" No. Sperm reach the fallopian tube within about five minutes.

    Egg Pick-Up and Fertilization

    • The oocyte is viable for about 12–24 hours after ovulation.
    • Sperm survive up to 5 days, but their fertilizing capacity probably lasts 2–3 days.
    • Fertilization happens in the ampulla, and may not be entirely random: there is evidence sperm are attracted to the egg.
    Part 2

    Infertility: The Basics

    Definitions, who to work up and when, and what age does to the numbers.

    Definitions and Numbers

    QuestionAnswer
    What is infertility?Regular, unprotected intercourse for a year without pregnancy. Frequency matters: if partners are apart for long stretches, or can't time intercourse around ovulation, the one-year rule means less
    Primary vs secondary?Primary: she has never been pregnant. Secondary: she has had a previous pregnancy (not necessarily a live birth). A partner's children from another relationship don't change it — that's still primary
    Who gets worked up early?≥35 → after 6 months (egg quantity and quality decline). Known problems → immediately: e.g. irregular cycles from PCOS, because without ovulation they won't conceive

    Age

    Fertility starts to decline around 30 and the decline accelerates. Both curves — falling fertility and rising miscarriage — are driven by the same thing: the age of the eggs.

    Part 3

    The Work-Up

    Six tests, each timed to a specific point in the cycle for a specific reason.

    The Standard Infertility Work-Up

    TestWhenWhat it answers
    Blood workDays 2–4 (day 1 = first day of bleeding)FSH, LH, estradiol, prolactin, TSH. Is the brain signalling the ovaries properly? Is prolactin or thyroid the problem?
    Sonohysterogram (+ ExEm foam)Days 6–12: lining is thin after the period, and there's no pregnancy yetSaline opens the cavity to look for polyps and fibroids; the foam shows whether the tubes are open; the ovaries are checked and follicles counted for egg reserve
    Day 21 progesteroneAbout a week before the expected periodDid she actually ovulate? After ovulation the follicle becomes the corpus luteum and makes progesterone. Even regular cycles aren't always ovulatory
    Semen analysisAny timeThe male half — see below
    Infectious disease screeningBoth partnersRequired by law in Ontario before using assisted reproductive technology. Best done at the fertility clinic: family doctors often miss tests like HTLV-1 and -2
    AMHAny time in the cycleEgg reserve, not natural fertility
    Why FSH, LH and Estradiol Are Always Ordered Together

    On day 2–4 everything should be low. If estradiol comes back at 500, that's far too high for early cycle: a dominant follicle is already growing, so the patient isn't where you think she is in her cycle.

    That matters because an FSH of 30 alone looks like diminished ovarian reserve or premature ovarian insufficiency. But if estradiol is also 500, the sample simply wasn't taken at the start of the cycle, and that FSH may be completely normal. LH is checked because it should be low at baseline; in PCOS it can already be high (up to about 18), which is why home ovulation strips are so hard to interpret in PCOS. Progesterone isn't part of day-3 bloods, unless the cycle phase is unknown or an ultrasound shows a complex cyst that might be a corpus luteum.

    Confirming Ovulation, and Timing Intercourse

    MethodHow it worksPractical?
    Mid-luteal progesteroneAbout 7 days before the expected period; a high level means ovulation happenedYes — the standard
    LH urine stripsTwo lines always appear; a true surge is when both lines are equally dark. Ovulation follows in about 36 hoursYes, and the surge day is the best day for intercourse
    Ultrasound monitoringA mature follicle, its collapse, free fluid, then a corpus luteumAccurate but needs daily scans
    Cervical mucusTwo consecutive days of clear, stretchy mucusVaries a lot between people
    Basal body temperatureA rise after ovulationHard to measure and track accurately

    What AMH does and doesn't tell you. It's made by granulosa cells of small follicles that aren't yet FSH-responsive, so it measures reserve. It does not predict natural fertility, even though patients panic about a low number. Its real use is guiding medication doses for IVF or egg-freezing.

    When Is a Cycle Actually Irregular?

    It depends on how long it's been since menarche.

    In the first year after menarche, irregular cycles are normal: the HPO axis is still immature.

    Part 4

    The Causes

    Ovulatory, tubal/peritoneal and male. Half of it is male factor.

    PCOS: The Commonest Ovulatory Disorder

    • Counsel on the symptoms: acne, excess hair, oily skin, hair loss, irregular cycles, infertility, weight gain, insulin resistance, mood changes.
    • And the long-term risks: cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, endometrial hyperplasia or cancer, and infertility.
    • Best management is preventing weight gain through lifestyle and regular exercise.
    • For fertility: ovulation induction, usually a pill that grows a follicle, sometimes with an injection (Ovidrel) to trigger release. The point is that PCOS patients usually need help to ovulate.
    • A 5–10% weight loss can restart spontaneous ovulation — a genuinely motivating number for patients.

    The full diagnostic work-up, including the mimics, is 3.10 PMOS.

    Endometriosis

    • Affects 30–50% of infertile women (with better imaging, this clinic suspects closer to 80%).
    • Gold standard: laparoscopy with biopsy. In practice most diagnoses are made on symptoms and response to treatment (combined pill or a progestin such as Visanne). Advanced ultrasound with pelvic mapping is increasingly useful.
    • Staging does not correlate with symptoms. Minimal disease can be debilitating; severe disease can be silent.
    TheoryIdea
    Sampson's retrograde menstruationThe most cited: menstrual tissue flows backwards through the tubes
    Embryonic restMisplaced embryonic Müllerian duct cells proliferate in the abdomen
    Coelomic metaplasiaPeritoneal cells, embryologically similar to endometrium, transform into it
    Halban'sLymphatic and vascular spread (lesions are found even in brain and lung)

    No single theory explains it; most researchers favour a multifactorial model: retrograde menstruation plus immune dysfunction, genetic susceptibility and environmental factors.

    Why Endometriosis Causes Infertility — Four Ways at Once

    Toxic fluid: the peritoneal fluid contains embryotoxic factors. Poor implantation: implantation rates are lower. Impaired follicles: ovarian reserve is often diminished and the eggs retrieved behave worse. Tubal transport: the pelvis sits in a chronic pro-inflammatory state that extends into the tubes, so sperm and egg have a harder time meeting. Add them up and you see why it fills the infertility clinic.

    Treating itApproach
    Not trying to conceiveHormonal suppression (it's estrogen-sensitive): combined pill, progestin-only pill, Visanne, progesterone IUD or implant, Depo-Provera. Second line: Orilissa or Lupron, which induce a chemical menopause
    Trying to conceive, stage I–IISuppression isn't possible. Laparoscopic ablation or excision may improve spontaneous pregnancy rates for up to 9 months after surgery
    Trying to conceive, stage III–IVIVF, which bypasses the toxic pelvic environment (implantation rates are still reduced)
    EndometriomasAvoid surgery in anyone wanting future fertility: it costs ovarian reserve. Operate only for severe symptoms, after careful risk–benefit counselling

    Male Factor: Half of It

    CauseDetail
    IdiopathicThe most common: no clear explanation
    VaricoceleSecond most common: enlarged scrotal veins impairing sperm production and quality
    Exogenous steroidsCommon in men using anabolic steroids. Testosterone acts as a male contraceptive: it suppresses the brain's drive, so spermatogenesis stops. Result: zero sperm on analysis
    Erectile / ejaculatory dysfunctionOften linked to cardiovascular disease or hypertension. Ask about sexual function
    InfectionsPast genital infections can obstruct the tract
    GeneticKlinefelter syndrome, Y chromosome microdeletions
    Radiation and chemotherapyDamage testicular tissue
    LifestyleMarijuana, smoking, hot tubs
    1
    Semen analysis first: count, motility, morphology.
    2
    If abnormal, hormones: LH, testosterone and 17-hydroxyprogesterone (which can pick up non-classical CAH).
    3
    If extremely abnormal, consider a karyotype.
    4
    Counsel about more than fertility: an abnormal semen analysis is associated with an increased lifetime cancer risk and earlier mortality.

    Male aging matters less than female aging, but it isn't nothing: semen volume, motility and morphology decline while concentration stays stable, and there are more new autosomal dominant mutations and higher rates of schizophrenia and autism in offspring. Male age is not associated with aneuploidy or sex chromosome abnormalities.

    Recurrent Pregnancy Loss

    Two or more losses — consecutive or not, including biochemical pregnancies. Two losses is enough to warrant a work-up. Fewer than 5% of couples have two consecutive losses, and fewer than 1% have three or more.

    CauseShareWhat to do
    Antiphospholipid antibody syndrome5–20%Diagnosed by three specific antibodies. If criteria are met, treat with heparin and aspirin from the first positive pregnancy test. This reduces loss, which is why ruling it in or out matters
    Uterine anomalies10–15%Septate, unicornuate or bicornuate uterus, or intrauterine polyps (see 3.7)
    Endocrine—Thyroid dysfunction, hyperprolactinemia, poorly controlled diabetes
    Chronic endometritis—A low-grade infection of the cavity; two weeks of antibiotics improves outcomes
    Genetic~5% of couplesA balanced reciprocal translocation in one partner produces unbalanced embryos, so karyotype both partners

    The work-up: endocrine bloodwork, ultrasound of the cavity (usually sonohysterogram), karyotypes of both partners, and an endometrial biopsy. Even so, about 50% have no identifiable cause, which is frustrating for everyone.
