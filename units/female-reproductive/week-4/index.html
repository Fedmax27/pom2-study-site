Week 4

Monday is ending a pregnancy, starting one, preventing one, and the drugs behind all of it. Tuesday turns to what comes through the clinic door: discharge, sexually transmitted infection, cervical screening, and the two early-pregnancy emergencies. Thursday is bleeding and pain — abnormal uterine bleeding and the vulva, then the same four benign lesions twice over, once under the microscope and once at the bedside, finishing with acute and chronic pelvic pain. Friday is prenatal genetic screening, from the family history before conception to amniocentesis. The physiology they rest on — the menstrual cycle and the HPO axis — is in 3.3 and 2.6.

4.1

Abortion in Canada

  • The law and access across Canada, medical abortion with mifepristone and misoprostol, surgical options (MVA, D&C, D&E), complications, the risk compared with pregnancy, contraception after, fertility and the myths→
4.2

Fertilization & Infertility

  • How fertilization happens and the fertile window, the definition and prevalence of infertility, causes in both partners, the family physician's work-up, and who needs early referral→
4.3

Contraception

  • Typical vs perfect use failure rates, the tiers of effectiveness, how each method works, LARCs, the estrogen contraindications, and emergency contraception→
4.4

Drugs Used in Gynecology

  • Estrogen and progesterone pharmacology, contraceptive products and their side effects and risks, SERMs and SPRMs, androgen agonists and antagonists, and the other drugs used in gynecology→
4.5

Vaginal Discharge

  • Why estrogen makes discharge, how it changes across the cycle, the assessment with pH, whiff test and wet mount, and the four causes — BV, candida, trichomoniasis and atrophic vaginitis→
4.6

Approach to Sexually Transmitted Infection

  • Chlamydia, gonorrhea and syphilis, hepatitis B serology and HIV, then pelvic inflammatory disease in depth — why it isn't always an STI, when to admit, and the 14-day rule→
4.7

Public Health and STIs

  • STBBIs, the rising local and provincial numbers, antimicrobial resistance in gonorrhea, risk factors, your legal duty to report, and why reducing stigma is itself a transmission-control measure→
4.8

Cervical Cancer Screening

  • HPV and its low- and high-risk types, the vaccines and schedules, cytology outcomes from NILM to HSIL, and Ontario's March 2025 switch to primary HPV screening with its algorithm→
4.9

Early Pregnancy Complications

  • Spontaneous abortion — the five types, the causes, and expectant vs medical vs surgical management — then ectopic pregnancy: risk factors, the discriminatory zone, methotrexate and when to operate→
4.10

Abnormal Uterine Bleeding

  • Building an approach instead of memorizing definitions: dividing by age, PALM-COEIN, anovulation down the HPO axis, reading the pelvic exam, the workup, the treatment ladder, and the two age extremes — adolescence and perimenopause→
4.11

Vulvar Lesions & Vulvar Cancer

  • Vulvar skin is skin: the three lichens separated on scarring, site and cancer risk, then melanosis, aphthae, vitiligo, molluscum, yeast vulvitis, Bartholin and lipoma masses, and the vulvar malignancies — SCC and VIN, melanoma, BCC and extramammary Paget→
4.12

Pathology of AUB & Pelvic Pain

  • Excluding structural causes before calling anything functional: structural vs functional, PALE, and the four benign lesions under the microscope — endometrial polyp, leiomyoma, adenomyosis and endometriosis — plus what to tell your patient about each→
4.13

Endometriosis

  • The history that separates it from primary dysmenorrhea, why severity doesn't track with pain, approaching dyspareunia, ovulatory suppression by pseudopregnancy or pseudomenopause, where surgery fits, and the infertility statistic everyone misreads→
4.14

Clinical Approach to Chronic Pelvic Pain

  • Chronic pain serves no physiologic purpose: the six-month definition, the differential across six organ systems, and five cases — primary dysmenorrhea, endometriosis, adenomyosis, fibroids and myofascial pelvic pain→
4.15

Clinical Approach to Acute Pelvic Pain

  • β-hCG in every woman of reproductive age, the pain history, and four must-know emergencies — ruptured ectopic, PID, ovarian torsion and ruptured hemorrhagic ovarian cyst→
4.16

Genetic Screening for Pregnancy in Canada

  • Screening is not diagnosis: the red-flag family history, fragile X and FMR1, ethnicity-based carrier screening, NT, eFTS and NIPT, CVS vs amniocentesis, timelines to 24 weeks, PGT, and a translocation trisomy 13 case→
