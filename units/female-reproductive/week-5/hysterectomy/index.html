    Hysterectomy Approaches

    If You Remember Nothing Else
    • Vaginal hysterectomy is the gold standard minimally invasive approach — it requires no abdominal incisions at all.
    • TLH and robotic replace an abdominal hysterectomy, never a vaginal one. If her anatomy allows a vaginal or LAVH approach, that comes first.
    • Only abdominal hysterectomy is not minimally invasive — 2–3 days in hospital. Every other approach can be a same-day discharge.
    • Total vs subtotal is about the cervix, not the ovaries. Total = uterus and cervix. Subtotal = cervix retained.
    • Hysterectomy alone does not cause surgical menopause. Only removing the ovaries does — which is usually what a patient asking for a "partial hysterectomy" actually means.
    • For bleeding control, medical management comes first. Hysterectomy is for failed medical management and special circumstances.

    The Vocabulary First

    TermWhat is removed
    Total hysterectomyUterus and cervix
    Subtotal hysterectomyUterus only — the cervix is retained
    + Salpingectomy… and the fallopian tubes
    + Salpingo-oophorectomy… and the fallopian tubes and ovaries
    "Partial hysterectomy" (lay term)Not a surgical term. Usually means removal of the uterus ± cervix ± tubes, with the ovaries retained
    The Conversation This Vocabulary Is Really About

    A hysterectomy alone does not cause surgical menopause. Only removing the ovaries does. So when a patient says she wants a "partial hysterectomy", what she is almost always asking is: can I keep my ovaries? Answering the question she asked, rather than correcting her terminology, is the point — and the consequences of getting it wrong for a woman under 40 are laid out in 5.3 under iatrogenic POI.

    When a hysterectomy is being considered for bleeding control, medical options should be tried first; hysterectomy is reserved for failed medical management and special circumstances. The medical ladder is in 4.10.

    The Five Approaches

    The ideal hysterectomy is done in a minimally invasive fashion. Four of the five approaches below qualify; one does not.

    ApproachMinimally invasive?LimitationsWhat it adds
    Vaginal
    The gold standard    		 Yes — no abdominal incisions at all. Short stay, same-day discharge Prior pelvic surgery / extensive scarring · large uterus (hard to manoeuvre out through the vagina) · narrow introitus or pelvis · tubes and ovaries not readily accessible from below · poor uterine descent or mobility, often in women who are nulliparous or have never been sexually active —
    LAVH
    Laparoscopic-assisted vaginal hysterectomy    		 Yes. Requires abdominal entry with laparoscopic instruments; part of the work is done vaginally. Same-day discharge possible Same as vaginal — very large uterus, poor uterine descent, narrow introitus or pelvis, because you still have to work within the vagina Assess the pelvis for scarring · create a bladder flap (dissect bladder off uterus and cervix) · helps with removal of tubes and ovaries · a "second look" for hemostasis through the existing ports once the vagina is closed
    TLH
    Total (or subtotal) laparoscopic hysterectomy    		 Yes. Requires abdominal entry. Same-day discharge possible Large uterus · laparoscopic suturing takes skill and more operative time than any vaginal approach Excellent when the vagina or pelvis won't allow work from below, or with poor uterine descent. Allows difficult dissection of a scarred bladder off the cervix, and removal of tubes and ovaries
    Robotic Yes. Requires abdominal entry. Same-day discharge possible Extremely large uterus · laparoscopic suturing still takes skill and time Articulating instruments that get around corners, so a larger uterus is more manageable than laparoscopically. An excellent option for patients with morbid obesity, who carry increased morbidity from a large incision
    Abdominal
    Via laparotomy    		 No. 2–3 day hospital stay Pfannenstiel (side-to-side) or midline vertical incision Reserved for more complex cancers, patient factors that rule out a minimally invasive approach, a very large uterus, or complications during an attempted minimally invasive approach
    The Hierarchy Is the Answer to the Module's Quiz

    Dr. Black states the rule twice, once for TLH and once for robotic: these are used in place of an abdominal hysterectomy, and should generally not replace a vaginal hysterectomy or LAVH. So the order is vaginal → LAVH → TLH / robotic → abdominal, and you move down it only when anatomy or pathology forces you to. Which also answers the module's knowledge check: the approach that cannot be done with the intention of same-day discharge is the abdominal one.

    Why "No Incisions" Beats "Better View"

    It is counterintuitive that the oldest and least technological approach sits at the top. The reason is that every advantage the laparoscopic and robotic approaches offer — visualization, bladder dissection, access to the adnexa — is bought with abdominal entry, and abdominal entry is exactly the morbidity that vaginal hysterectomy avoids entirely. The newer approaches earn their place by making a laparotomy unnecessary, not by making a vaginal hysterectomy obsolete.
