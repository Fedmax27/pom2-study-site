    Topics of Antenatal Care

    If You Remember Nothing Else
    • Pregnancy adds ~350–450 kcal/day. Healthy eating matters more than weight targets; everyone takes a prenatal multivitamin. The two supplements most often needed: folic acid and iron.
    • Live attenuated vaccines (MMR, varicella, BCG) are the ones to avoid — the risk is theoretical, so an inadvertent dose is not a disaster.
    • Every pregnancy gets Tdap at 27–32 weeks (antibodies cross the placenta to protect the newborn) and influenza vaccine at any gestation in flu season.
    • Rh-negative? Rh immunoglobulin routinely at 28–30 weeks and postpartum if the baby is Rh-positive, plus after bleeding, an invasive procedure or trauma from 8–10 weeks.
    • A maternal antibody: titre, find the cause, test the partner. Titre ≥ 1:8 is critical; below it, repeat monthly. Above it, watch the fetus with MCA peak systolic velocity.
    • Sensitized and stable: deliver at 37–38 weeks. Severe anemia after 34–35 weeks: deliver. Severe anemia earlier: intrauterine transfusion.

    Nutrition

    Needs rise because the uterus grows dramatically, the breasts prepare for feeding, and blood volume and red-cell mass expand to fill a much larger circulation. On average that is 350–450 extra kcal/day: less with a high BMI, more if she was underweight. Focus on healthy eating, not specific foods or a weight-gain number, and recommend a daily prenatal multivitamin to everyone.

    Vaccinations in Pregnancy

    Most vaccines are safe in pregnancy, and some are actively recommended. Their safety comes from decades of observational data.

    CategoryVaccinesWhat to do
    Recommended in every pregnancyTdap (tetanus, diphtheria, acellular pertussis)At 27–32 weeks — maternal IgG crosses the placenta and gives the newborn passive immunity to pertussis
    InfluenzaAny gestational age, or postpartum, in flu season — cuts maternal severity and transmission to the newborn
    Indicated in select situations (inactivated)Hepatitis A · hepatitis B · Haemophilus influenzae b · meningococcus · rabiesGive if she would need it anyway — pregnancy is not the reason to withhold it
    Avoid — defer to postpartumLive attenuated: MMR, varicella, BCGTheoretical risk only: if attenuation failed, a live organism could infect her and cross to the fetus. Realistically very rare, so if risk/benefit favours vaccinating, it can be considered
    The Word That Decides It: “Live”

    An attenuated vaccine is the living organism, weakened so it no longer causes disease. That is why it is the one class held back: in theory it could still replicate in a pregnant woman. Inactivated vaccines, toxoids and subunit vaccines cannot. So the exam question “which vaccine is contraindicated?” nearly always resolves to the live one — MMR. That is also why a rubella-non-immune woman found on her first-trimester bloods is vaccinated postpartum (5.12).

    Group & Screen and Rh Immunoglobulin

    Every pregnancy gets a group and screen in the first trimester, for three reasons: her blood type (any delivery can need a transfusion for hemorrhage), her Rh status, and any red-cell antibodies already present.

    Rh-negative woman: when to give Rh immunoglobulinWhy then
    Routinely at 28–30 weeksCovers the rest of the pregnancy
    Postpartum, if the baby is Rh-positiveDelivery is the biggest fetomaternal bleed
    Any time after 8–10 weeks with vaginal bleeding, an invasive procedure (CVS, amniocentesis) or traumaEach can disrupt the placental interface and let fetal cells into her circulation
    Every 12 weeks if still undeliveredOne dose protects for about 12 weeks
    How It Works — and Why the First Pregnancy Is Usually Spared

    Rh immunoglobulin clears fetal red cells from her circulation before her immune system recognizes them, so she never makes her own antibodies. When sensitization does happen, the first response is mostly IgM, which is too large to cross the placenta well. In a later pregnancy, the established response is IgG, which is small, crosses easily, and is present from conception. That gives it the whole pregnancy to act. Hence the lecture's rule: the antibody generally has to be there at the start of the pregnancy to cause trouble.

    What sensitizes a woman (the module's knowledge check): fetal cells entering her blood in a previous pregnancy or invasive procedure, a blood transfusion, and shared needles in IV drug use. All three count.

    When She Already Has an Antibody

    Why the MCA, and Why Deliver at 37–38 Weeks

    Anemic blood carries less oxygen, so the fetus protects its brain by pumping blood there faster. A rising MCA peak systolic velocity therefore tracks falling hemoglobin without needling the fetus. Once the titre is above the critical level (or started very high), further titres add nothing, so you watch the fetus instead. Delivering a stable sensitized pregnancy at 37–38 weeks avoids a late escalation of the response. It also avoids a practical problem: near term the denser skull and a low head in the pelvis make the MCA hard to measure.
