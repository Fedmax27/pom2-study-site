Week 5

Monday is the other end of the reproductive lifespan: bleeding after menopause and the endometrial pathology behind it, the pelvic mass under the microscope, the clinical management of menopause itself, and the two operations that keep coming up — D&C and hysterectomy. Tuesday is the breast, twice over — the pathology underneath and the three complaints that actually walk into a family practice. Thursday is early pregnancy from four directions — who provides the care, what harms a fetus, what the ultrasound and β-hCG actually show, and what the tissue looks like. Friday turns to antenatal care, hypertension in pregnancy and sexual health. The hormone physiology underneath Monday is in 3.3 and 2.6; the bleeding and pain framework it builds on is 4.10 and 4.12.

Monday

Sept 28 The other end of the reproductive lifespan — bleeding after menopause, the pelvic mass, menopause itself, and the two operations that keep coming up.
5.1

Post-menopausal Bleeding

  • The 12-month definition, the TVUS-and-biopsy pathway with its 5 mm cutoff, four biopsy reports and what each one means, atrophy, hyperplasia with and without atypia, and endometrial carcinoma from Lynch syndrome to staging→
5.2

Approach to & Pathology of a Pelvic Mass

  • Every ovarian cell type and the tumour it makes: epithelial benign to borderline to malignant, the tubal origin of high-grade serous carcinoma, teratomas and germ cell markers, fibroma and granulosa cell tumour, and the metastases that masquerade as a primary→
5.3

Clinical Approach to Menopause

  • Menopause, perimenopause and premature ovarian insufficiency; KNDy neurons and vasomotor symptoms; genitourinary syndrome of menopause and its treatment ladder; menopausal hormone therapy, its window and its contraindications; the non-hormonal options; and a three-part case→
5.4

Dilation & Curettage

  • Vacuum aspiration to 14 weeks, cervical preparation with misoprostol or osmotic dilators, the procedure step by step, and the complications — perforation, hemorrhage, hematometra, retained products and Asherman's syndrome→
5.5

Hysterectomy Approaches

  • Total vs subtotal, what "partial hysterectomy" actually means, and the five approaches ranked — vaginal, LAVH, laparoscopic, robotic and abdominal — with the limitations that push you down the list→

Tuesday

Sept 29 The breast, twice over — the pathology underneath, then the three complaints that actually walk into a family practice.
5.6

Pathology of the Breast

  • One diagram that maps every lesion to the structure it arises from; the myoepithelial layer that separates in situ from invasive; fat necrosis, mastitis, papilloma, fibroadenoma and fibrocystic change; the three-tier epithelial risk table; and breast cancer from BRCA to DCIS versus LCIS to ER, PR and HER2→
5.7

Approach to Common Breast Problems

  • The three complaints that walk in — a lump, soreness, nipple discharge — with the clinical breast exam, the worrisome features, ultrasound under 35 and mammogram over it, cyclical versus non-cyclical pain, pathologic discharge, and screening from OBSP and the 1-in-8 lifetime risk to what mammography actually costs→

Thursday

Oct 1 Early pregnancy from four directions — who provides the care, what harms a fetus, what the ultrasound and β-hCG show, and what the tissue looks like.
5.8

OB Care Providers

  • Family medicine OB, midwives and obstetricians side by side — training, scope, oxytocin and epidurals, who looks after the baby, informed choice versus informed consent, and when to consult an obstetrician antepartum, intrapartum and postpartum→
5.9

Teratogenesis

  • Why timing decides the damage — the critical-periods chart, the all-or-nothing first fortnight and organogenesis at weeks 3 to 8 — the four categories of teratogen, the FDA risk classification, and counselling before, during and after pregnancy→
5.10

First Trimester Bleeding & Ultrasound

  • Dating by ultrasound rather than dates — gestational sac, yolk sac, crown–rump length and fetal heart in sequence — then β-hCG: how fast a viable pregnancy really rises, why a normal rise doesn't exclude an ectopic, and the case for raising the discriminatory zone from 1500 to 2000–3000→
5.11

Pathology of First Trimester Bleeding

  • Placental implantation and the three trophoblasts; what tissue proves an intrauterine gestation and what a tubal ectopic looks like down the microscope; then gestational trophoblastic disease in full — complete versus partial mole, and choriocarcinoma→

Friday

Oct 2 The pregnancy from booking visit to the anatomy scan, and the complication that can end it early — plus how to talk about sex.
5.12

Care for Pregnancy in the First Trimester

  • Diagnosing pregnancy and the β-hCG thresholds; dating by the first ultrasound, not the LMP, with Naegele's rule as the rough check; GTPAL with worked examples; symptoms and fundal height through the first 12 weeks; and the booking bloodwork everyone gets→
5.13

Approach to Sexual Health

  • Masters & Johnson, Kaplan and Basson's circle; the male and female response phase by phase; taking a sexual history with the 5 Ps; making the office a safe space; and the terms for gender and orientation→
5.14

Topics of Antenatal Care

  • Extra calories, folate and iron; which vaccines are recommended (Tdap, influenza) and which to avoid (live ones); Rh immunoglobulin timing; and managing a red-cell antibody from titre to MCA Doppler to intrauterine transfusion→
5.15

Second Trimester Ultrasound

  • The 18–22 week anatomy scan everyone is offered, and its five jobs — growth, number of fetuses, placental location, the anatomy survey, and soft markers for trisomy 21 and 18→
5.16

Hypertension in Pregnancy

  • 140/90 and 160/110; why 20 weeks separates chronic from gestational hypertension; preeclampsia, eclampsia and HELLP; adverse conditions and severe complications by organ system; labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa, magnesium sulphate — and why ACE inhibitors are out→
