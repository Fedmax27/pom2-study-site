Week 6

Monday is the third trimester going wrong before labour: contractions that come too early, a baby that isn't growing, and bleeding from the placenta. Tuesday is labour itself: how a normal labour and birth unfold, what to do when labour stalls or needs starting, forceps, vacuum, caesarean and labouring after one, postpartum hemorrhage, and resuscitating a woman in shock. Thursday is the first weeks after birth: the newborn (APGAR, the exam, keeping babies warm and fed, jaundice), the mother's recovery and mood, and breastfeeding. The antenatal care these build on is in Week 5, especially the anatomy scan (5.15) and hypertension in pregnancy (5.16).

Monday

Oct 5 Too early, too small, bleeding — three ways the third trimester goes wrong.
6.1

Approach to Preterm Labour

  • Threatened preterm labour vs preterm labour vs preterm birth; the five etiologies and the risk factors; one patient followed from triage to the NICU; tocolysis, betamethasone, magnesium for neuroprotection and GBS prophylaxis, with their 32- and 34-week cut-offs; and PPROM from nitrazine and ferning to latency antibiotics→
6.2

Approach to the Small Fetus

  • The 10th and 3rd percentiles, SGA versus IUGR, and low, very low and extremely low birth weight; symphysis–fundal height and the growth-scan schedule; maternal, fetal and placental causes; the umbilical artery Doppler from rising resistance to absent and reversed flow; and when to deliver→
6.3

Antepartum Hemorrhage & Abnormal Placentation

  • Abruption, previa and vasa previa side by side — painful, painless, fetal blood; the accreta spectrum after a caesarean; why you never examine the cervix before the ultrasound; the stat bloodwork, Kleihauer–Betke and Apt tests; when to deliver; and how bleeding becomes a consumptive coagulopathy→

Tuesday

Oct 6 Labour and birth — the normal version, everything that goes wrong with it, and the shock at the end.
6.4

Process of Labour & Birth

  • The four stages and Friedman's timings; intermittent versus continuous monitoring and the SOGC normal–atypical–abnormal tracing; early, variable and late decelerations; intrauterine resuscitation; dilatation, effacement, station, lie, presentation and Leopold's; the seven cardinal movements; the third stage; and perineal tears from first to fourth degree→
6.5

Abnormal Labour & Birth

  • Labour dystocia and the 4 Ps; induction from the Bishop score to prostaglandins, Foley, ARM and oxytocin, with what the ARRIVE trial actually showed; forceps versus vacuum; caesarean indications, incisions and risks; cord prolapse; TOLAC and uterine rupture; and postpartum hemorrhage — the 4 Ts, prevention, and a step-by-step management flowchart→
6.6

Hemorrhagic Shock

  • The four classes of hemorrhage and why blood pressure falls last; why young and postpartum women look well until they don't; the resuscitation sequence and massive transfusion; the lethal triad of acidosis, hypothermia and coagulopathy; and a ruptured ectopic worked through step by step→

Thursday

Oct 8 After the birth — the newborn, the mother, and feeding.
6.7

Approach to Neonatal Care

  • The APGAR score and a worked example; the newborn exam and the Ballard score; four ways a baby loses heat; hypoglycemia — who to screen, “2.6, no more sticks”, and when it's an emergency; bilirubin from heme to excretion, the screening curves, red flags, kernicterus and phototherapy; global neonatal mortality and Helping Babies Survive→
6.8

Postpartum Care

  • Discharge criteria; perineal and bowel care; when the uterus, cervix, endometrium and blood volume return to normal; ovulation and contraception after birth; sleep; blues vs postpartum depression vs psychosis, with PHQ-2, GAD-2 and EPDS; delayed PPH, puerperal infection, endometritis and VTE; the 6-week visit→
6.9

Lactation

  • Breast anatomy; prolactin, oxytocin and milk removal, endocrine to autocrine control; colostrum and the benefits of breast milk; feeding and the latch; mastitis and abscess; low supply; alcohol, drugs and contraindications; storage, weaning, cabergoline and safe contraception→
